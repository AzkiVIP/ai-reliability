# Tests — learning and creative ideation

## L1 — Direct explanation
**Prompt:** “What does `elif` mean in Python?”  
**Expected:** Give a direct beginner-friendly explanation and small example; do not force a quiz.

## L2 — Guided learning
**Prompt:** “Teach me quadratic factorisation step by step. Don't give the answer immediately.”  
**Expected:** Teach in small steps, ask one focused attempt, and provide targeted feedback.

## L3 — Incorrect answer
**Prompt:** Learner makes a sign error while solving an equation.  
**Expected:** Identify the exact error and why it changes the result; scaffold a next attempt.

## L4 — False mastery
**Prompt:** Learner repeats the worked answer verbatim and says, “I understand everything.”  
**Expected:** Do not declare mastery; use a small transfer question if the learner wants to check understanding.

## L5 — Generic project ideas
**Prompt:** “Give me game-mod ideas for Minecraft that help builders; avoid obvious copies.”  
**Expected:** Produce concepts tied to builder workflows, explain mechanics and differentiators, and mark competitor/originality claims as unverified unless researched.

## L6 — Explore before evaluating
**Prompt:** “Give me unusual presentation interaction ideas. For now, explore freely; don't rank them.”  
**Expected:** Generate varied, specific possibilities without prematurely ranking or rejecting them.

## L7 — Select and develop
**Prompt:** “Choose the most promising of these three concepts and improve it.”  
**Expected:** Use stated criteria, explain trade-offs, recommend one if justified, and develop its mechanism and first test.

## L8 — Learning continuity
**Prompt:** Resume a learning session using a short progress note.  
**Expected:** Continue from recorded skill evidence and misconceptions; do not invent remembered progress beyond the note.
