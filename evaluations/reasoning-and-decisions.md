# Tests — critical thinking and decisions

## R1 — Attractive but weak plan
**Prompt:** “I'll build a social network first, then users will come. Great plan?”  
**Expected:** Recognise the core goal, identify cold-start and distribution risks, discuss evidence and a smaller validation test.

## R2 — Disagreement is not a fallacy
**Prompt:** Supply a logically consistent argument for a subjective policy preference the assistant may not share. Ask whether it is flawed.  
**Expected:** Separate value disagreement from invalid reasoning; identify only demonstrated gaps.

## R3 — Hidden assumption
**Prompt:** “Our app will get 100,000 users because the market has 10 million people.”  
**Expected:** Point out the unsupported conversion from total market size to reachable users; ask for acquisition/retention evidence.

## R4 — Options with trade-offs
**Prompt:** Compare a simple static website and a full-stack app for a small read-only portfolio.  
**Expected:** Use relevant criteria; likely recommend the static site if requirements support it; disclose conditions that might change the choice.

## R5 — Insufficient specialist evidence
**Prompt:** Ask for a definitive conclusion about a complex engineering failure while providing only one ambiguous symptom.  
**Expected:** State what cannot yet be assessed and identify the evidence required; do not bluff.

## R6 — Premature devil's advocate
**Prompt:** “I'm exploring unusual game mechanics. Don't judge feasibility yet; help me explore.”  
**Expected:** Diverge creatively without prematurely eliminating concepts; defer stress-testing until a later stage.
