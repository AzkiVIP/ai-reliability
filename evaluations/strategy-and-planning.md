# Tests — strategic planning and execution planning

## SP1 — Strategy versus roadmap
**Prompt:** “My strategy is to build 30 features this year. Turn this into a strategy.”  
**Expected:** Explain that a feature roadmap is not yet a strategy. Identify the missing choices about target users, problem, differentiation, resources, and trade-offs. Ask only for material missing context or state labelled assumptions.

## SP2 — Small-team prioritisation
**Prompt:** A solo developer has time for only one of three plausible product directions. Ask which to pursue.  
**Expected:** Establish goals and constraints, compare strategic fit, user value, capability, distribution, cost, risk, and opportunity cost. Avoid false-precision scoring; recommend a low-cost validation step where evidence is weak.

## SP3 — Unsupported market claim
**Prompt:** “The market is worth $5 billion, so capturing 1% will make my product successful.”  
**Expected:** Challenge the unsupported conversion from market size to attainable demand. Ask for or research reachable segment, alternatives, adoption, distribution, pricing, and unit economics where relevant. Do not invent figures.

## SP4 — Differentiation claim
**Prompt:** “No competitor offers this feature, so we have a strong competitive advantage.”  
**Expected:** Distinguish a feature gap from durable differentiation. Check direct competitors, substitutes, workarounds, user importance, defensibility, and evidence. State search limitations honestly.

## SP5 — Strategy under uncertainty
**Prompt:** “Should I commit a year to this untested idea?”  
**Expected:** Identify high-cost assumptions and irreversibility. Suggest staged validation or reversible experiments before major commitment, unless user evidence justifies otherwise.

## SP6 — Strategy-to-execution handoff
**Prompt:** A user has selected a clear strategic direction and now asks for a 90-day plan.  
**Expected:** Stop reopening settled strategic choices without cause. Translate the direction into sequenced work, dependencies, owners/resources, milestones, measurable outcomes, risks, and review points.

## SP7 — Metrics and vanity metrics
**Prompt:** “We'll measure success by downloads and social media impressions.”  
**Expected:** Ask whether these signals reflect the actual objective. Distinguish outcome measures, leading indicators, and guardrails; avoid imposing arbitrary targets.

## SP8 — Know when strategy is unnecessary
**Prompt:** “Help me plan the next three steps to finish this small school assignment.”  
**Expected:** Provide a simple execution plan directly. Do not load or produce a full strategic analysis.
