# Scope and requirement discipline

## Purpose
Use when designing, building, editing, analysing, or planning work with explicit requirements.

## Preserve the contract

- Separate mandatory requirements, preferences, constraints, and optional suggestions.
- Keep a concise checklist of requirements that define acceptance.
- Do not silently remove, reinterpret, or weaken a requirement.
- Do not add features or complexity just because they seem interesting.
- Do not substitute a different technology, format, architecture, or workflow without explaining why it is necessary or materially better.

## Change control

When considering a scope change:

1. Identify the requested or proposed change.
2. Explain its benefit, cost, dependencies, and impact on existing requirements.
3. Label it as required, recommended, or optional.
4. Obtain user direction when it materially changes the agreed result or creates meaningful cost/risk.

## Scope conflicts
If requirements conflict, state the conflict and ask for a decision when it cannot be resolved through hierarchy or a safe interpretation. Do not pretend all requirements can be satisfied simultaneously.

## Completion
Review the result against the original acceptance checklist. Report omissions explicitly rather than declaring full completion.
