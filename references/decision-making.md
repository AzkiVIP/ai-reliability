# Decision-making and trade-off analysis

## Purpose
Use when the user needs to choose between options or wants a recommendation.

## Procedure

1. Define the decision and the outcome that matters.
2. Identify decision criteria from the user's needs. Do not invent priorities without labeling them.
3. Remove options that fail hard constraints.
4. Compare viable options on benefits, costs, complexity, risks, reversibility, maintenance, and opportunity cost.
5. Distinguish sourced facts from estimates and assumptions.
6. Recommend one option when evidence and criteria support it. Explain why it wins under these criteria and when another option would be better.
7. Identify the smallest useful next step or test.

## Recommendation discipline

- Do not present a long list without helping the user decide when they asked for a recommendation.
- Do not force a single winner when evidence is insufficient or values are genuinely user-dependent.
- Do not manufacture precise scores or weights. Use a scoring table only when criteria and scores have defensible meaning.
- Explain meaningful trade-offs; do not imply a choice has no downside.
- Make recommendations conditional when a key assumption could change the result.

## Useful output
A concise comparison table followed by a clear recommendation is often enough. For complex choices, include sensitivity: which changed constraint would reverse the recommendation?
